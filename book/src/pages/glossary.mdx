# Glossary & Scholarly References

A comprehensive reference for clinical terms, mathematical formulas, and scientific literature cited throughout *Switch Scanning: The Interactive Book*.

---

## Technical & Clinical Terms

<h3 id="glossary-term-acceptance-time">Acceptance Time (Acceptance Delay)</h3>
<p>The duration (in milliseconds) a switch must be continuously held down before an activation is confirmed by the system. Used to filter out unintended tremors, spasms, or accidental taps. Typical range: 150–600 ms.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-auto-scan">Auto-Scan (Single Switch Scanning)</h3>
<p>A system-paced scanning mode where the software automatically moves the highlight across items sequentially at a fixed <em>Scan Rate</em> (T_scan). The user waits for the desired target and depresses the single switch to make a selection.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-coefficient-of-variation">Coefficient of Variation (CV)</h3>
<p>A statistical measure of motor consistency defined as <code>CV = SD ÷ RT</code>, where <code>SD</code> is the standard deviation of reaction times and <code>RT</code> is the mean reaction time. When <code>CV &gt; 0.3</code>, extra timing buffers or acceptance delays are recommended.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-continuous-scan">Continuous Scanning</h3>
<p>A scanning technique where a selection cursor or crosshair moves smoothly across an analog space (or grid) rather than stepping discretely item-by-item. Requires precise timing control.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-critical-overscan">Critical Overscan</h3>
<p>A two-stage scanning technique featuring a rapid forward pass (100–200 ms per item) followed by a slow backward pass (1000–1500 ms per item) upon initial switch activation, allowing fast visual scanning with high selection accuracy.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-debounce">Debounce Delay</h3>
<p>A hardware or software filter that ignores rapid electrical contact spikes (mechanical bounce) for a brief window (typically 50–100 ms) immediately following switch activation.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-directed-scan">Directed Scanning</h3>
<p>A user-paced scanning method where the user directly controls highlight movement direction (Up, Down, Left, Right) using a joystick or multi-switch array.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-dwell-time">Dwell Time</h3>
<p>An automatic selection mechanism where an item is selected if the scanning highlight or gaze point remains on it uninterrupted for a designated threshold duration (e.g., 1000–1500 ms) without requiring a physical switch press.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-elimination-scan">Elimination Scanning (Quadrant Scanning)</h3>
<p>A hierarchical scanning pattern that recursively partitions a selection set of N items into m equal subgroups (e.g., 4 quadrants). The average number of selections required is <code>S = log_m(N)</code>.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-first-item-delay">1st-Item Delay (First Item Pause)</h3>
<p>An extended pause applied specifically to the first item of a row or column during scanning. Gives the user extra time to recognize that a new group has been entered before making a selection.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-input-bandwidth">Input Bandwidth</h3>
<p>The rate of information transmission achievable by a user, measured in bits per second or words per minute (WPM). Switch access operates at the lowest end of the human input bandwidth spectrum (1 binary bit per activation).</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-koester-decision-model">Koester & Simpson Decision Model (2014)</h3>
<p>An evidence-based clinical algorithm for optimizing scanning parameters. Its core rule is the <strong>25% Error Threshold</strong>: if scanning error rate exceeds 25%, all interventions must prioritize error reduction (slower scan delay, 1st-item delay, acceptance delay) before optimizing speed or layout.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-row-column">Row-Column Scanning</h3>
<p>A two-pass matrix scanning technique where Stage 1 scans rows sequentially and Stage 2 scans individual items within the selected row. For an R × C matrix, average scan steps per selection <code>S = (R + 1) ÷ 2 + (C + 1) ÷ 2</code>.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-scan-rate">Scan Rate (Scan Delay, T_scan)</h3>
<p>The duration (in milliseconds) that each item or group remains highlighted before the auto-scan advances to the next target.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-selection-set">Selection Set (N)</h3>
<p>The total collection of choices, symbols, letters, or commands available for selection on an interface at a given time.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-step-scan">Step Scanning (Two-Switch Scanning)</h3>
<p>A user-paced scanning mode utilizing two switches: Switch 1 (Advance) manually moves the highlight to the next item, and Switch 2 (Select) confirms the selection. Eliminates timing pressure.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-text-entry-rate">Text Entry Rate (TER)</h3>
<p>The net communication speed measured in Words Per Minute: <code>WPM = (Characters ÷ 5) ÷ Minutes</code>. Accounts for scanning delay, layout cost, and error correction overhead.</p>

<h3 id="glossary-term-dot65-rule">The .65 Rule</h3>
<p>An empirical heuristic formulated by Simpson & Koester (1999) stating that the optimal starting scan rate is <code>T_scan = Mean Reaction Time ÷ 0.65 ≈ 1.54 × RT</code>, providing a ~54% safety margin against timing errors.</p>

## Scholarly Literature & Key References

1. **Koester, H. H., & Simpson, R. C. (2014).** Method for enhancing text entry rate with single-switch scanning. *Journal of Rehabilitation Research and Development (JRRD)*, 51(6), 995–1012. [DOI: 10.1682/JRRD.2013.08.0184]
   - *Key finding:* Systematic clinical optimization of scanning settings yielded a **120% average increase in Text Entry Rate (TER)** (from 1.42 WPM to 2.72 WPM up to 6.51 WPM). Established the 25% error threshold rule.

2. **Simpson, R. C., & Koester, H. H. (2013).** Development and validation of Scanning Wizard: Software to optimize switch scanning settings. *Augmentative and Alternative Communication*, 29(4), 341–353.
   - *Key finding:* Validated automated software heuristics for calculating personalized scan rate, 1st-item delay, and acceptance delay for single-switch users.

3. **Simpson, R. C., & Koester, H. H. (1999).** Adaptive one-switch row-column scanning. *IEEE Transactions on Rehabilitation Engineering*, 7(4), 464–473.
   - *Key finding:* Introduced the **.65 Rule** (`T_scan = RT / 0.65`) for setting baseline scan delays based on user reaction time distributions.

4. **Damper, R. I. (1984).** Text composition by the physically disabled: A review of scanning techniques. *International Journal of Man-Machine Studies*, 21(6), 501–516.
   - *Key finding:* Early foundational mathematical models of scanning cost, row-column matrix efficiency, and text entry rates for physical access devices.

5. **Lesher, G. W., Moulton, B. J., & Higginbotham, D. J. (1998).** Techniques for augmenting scanning communication. *Augmentative and Alternative Communication*, 14(2), 81–101.
   - *Key finding:* Detailed analysis of word prediction list lengths, keystroke savings, and visual search overhead in AAC scanning systems.

6. **Fitts, P. M. (1951).** The information capacity of the human motor system in controlling the amplitude of movement. *Journal of Experimental Psychology*, 47(6), 381–391.
   - *Key finding:* Fitts's Law foundation for human-computer interaction, input bandwidth, and target selection dynamics.
